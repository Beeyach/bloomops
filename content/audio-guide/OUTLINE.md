# Where Your Messages Go

A short audio guide for coaches, clinics, and therapists that run on bookings.
Read by Ary, in her voice, generated on ElevenLabs with her cloned voice and
her audit-video settings (turbo model, her saved dials).

Purpose: lead magnet. Given away to the exact people Bloomwired prospects,
in the exact voice the cold emails already use. A coach can listen to the
whole thing on one commute.

Format: eight chapters, five to seven minutes each. Around 45,000 to 55,000
characters total, which is a quarter of one month's ElevenLabs allowance.
Quality over meter: nothing gets generated that Ary has not read.

Voice rules (from the ary-voice teaching register, adapted for audio):
- Talk to one person. "You" and "your business", never "business owners".
- Plain words. No metaphors, no imagery, no corporate verbs, no em dashes.
- One statistic in the whole guide, flagged as exactly that.
- Honest asides before the listener could wonder. Money, limits, motives.
- Verdict sentences after explanations. Short ones.
- Print devices become spoken signposts: "Here is the one line to keep."
- Numbers written out as words, because the voice reads them.

## Chapters

1. **Where your messages go.** The thesis. Can I tell you what I see every
   week? The owner cares, she is just doing every job herself. The one
   statistic: most people ask two or three businesses at once, and the first
   clear reply usually gets the conversation.
2. **The hour after they press send.** Reply speed. What a person does while
   they wait, and how long they actually wait before asking someone else.
3. **The form on your website.** What we can and cannot see from outside.
   What someone sees after submitting, and the ten-second check the owner
   can do herself. If that is already handled, this chapter is a victory lap.
4. **Booking without the back-and-forth.** The calendar that answers for
   you. Four messages to agree on a time versus a link that shows the times.
5. **The follow-up nobody sends.** Nobody forgets on purpose. Busy days win
   sometimes. The quote that went quiet and what one plain follow-up does.
6. **Reminders, and the people who do not show.** The no-show is usually a
   reminder problem, not a person problem.
7. **The list you already own.** Past clients. The cheapest booking you will
   ever get is someone who already trusts you.
8. **Doing this without sounding like a robot.** Where the line is. Honesty
   as the whole trick. The escape hatch. The worst case, stated plainly, as
   the close of the guide.

# Chapter 1. Where your messages go

Hi. I'm Ary.

I build booking and follow-up systems for small businesses. In plain words, I help owners reply to every inquiry fast and follow up on every one, so fewer people go quiet.

This is a short guide. Eight chapters, a few minutes each. You can listen while you drive, or between clients, or while you fold laundry. Nothing in it needs a screen.

Before we start, an honest note. I run a studio called Bloomwired, and we sell the kind of setup this guide talks about. I'd rather tell you that now than have you wonder about it in chapter five. But here's the thing. Everything in this guide works whether you ever talk to us or not. Most of it you can check or fix yourself, this week, for free. I'll tell you plainly when something is the sort of job people pay us for, and that's it. No tricks in the middle.

Okay.

Can I tell you what I see every week?

I look at websites for a living. Coaches, clinics, therapists, studios. Most weeks I look at forty or fifty of them, properly. Not a glance. I fill in the forms. I press the buttons. I read what comes back.

And most of these websites are good. Honestly, better than people expect. The photos are warm. The services are clear. The owner has thought about what she offers and who it's for. You can tell someone cares.

Then I press send on the contact form, and everything goes quiet.

No page saying it worked. No email saying it arrived. No hint of when a reply might come, or from whom. The website did its whole job, it got a stranger to raise their hand, and then the conversation just stops.

That's the part nobody sees. The owner doesn't see it, because she's on the other side of it. The visitor sees it once, waits a while, and asks someone else.

Here's what I want you to hold onto. The owner is not lazy. The owner cares more than almost anyone. She's with a client, or driving, or done for the day. She's doing every job herself, and answering messages is the job that has no set hour, so it happens last, or it happens tomorrow, or the message lands under three others and it happens never.

Nobody forgets on purpose. Busy days win sometimes.

That's okay. Truly. This guide is not here to make you feel bad about a message you missed in March. It's here because this problem has a boring, fixable shape, and almost nobody fixes it, and the ones who do get clients their competitors never even knew existed.

Now. This guide has exactly one statistic in it, and this is it. When a person wants a coach, or a physio, or a massage, they don't usually message one business. They message two or three, in the same sitting, usually in the same ten minutes. And then, most of the time, they go with whoever replies first with something clear.

Not whoever is cheapest. Not whoever has the nicest website. Whoever answered.

That's the whole game, and almost nobody is playing it.

Sit with that for a second, because it changes what you should worry about. You don't need a prettier website. You don't need to post more. The people are already coming. They're pressing send on your form right now, this week. The question is only what happens in the hour after they press it.

So that's what this guide walks through. The hour after the form. The booking that takes four messages when it could take zero. The follow-up nobody sends. The reminder that would have saved the no-show. The past clients sitting in your phone who would book again if anyone asked.

None of it is clever. I want to say that clearly, because there are a lot of people who will sell you clever. Funnels, automations with twelve steps, software with a dashboard you'll open twice. You don't need most of it. What you need is for every person who reaches out to get a fast, clear, human answer, every time, including the times you're busy. That's it. That's the entire book.

One more honest thing, then we'll get into it. Some people feel like setting up automatic replies and reminders is cheating. Like it's less personal. I don't. What's less personal is silence. A person who hears back in two minutes feels taken care of. A person who hears nothing for two days feels like a number, even if the delay happened because you were busy taking care of someone else.

The reply can be warm and automatic at the same time. We'll get there.

Here's the one line to keep from this chapter. People are already choosing you. The only question is whether anything answers when they do.

Okay. Let's go find out where your messages have been going.
